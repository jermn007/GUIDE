# Practice: Score This, Then Compare

Reading a rubric is not the same as using one. These three short exercises ask you to score something yourself first, then compare your scores with GUIDE's and read why. Each takes about five minutes. A short review schedule at the end brings the main ideas back over the following week, because one pass does not stick.

Have the rubric open while you score: [`archetypes/`](../archetypes) holds one `handoff_NN_*.md` file per archetype, and the printable rubric has the same text.

## Part 1: Score these

Write your scores down before you look at Part 2. A score you only think about is easy to revise after the fact.

### Exercise 1: a quiz (archetype 02, Assessment Design)

A trainer sends you this and says it is the complete end-of-module quiz for new baristas. The procedure as taught has four steps: purge the wand, stretch the milk, texture the milk, then wipe and purge the wand.

> **Objective:** List the four steps of the milk-steaming procedure in order.
>
> **Q1.** Which step comes first? a) Purge the wand b) Stretch the milk c) Wipe the wand d) Texture the milk
>
> **Q2.** Which step comes last? a) Purge the wand b) Stretch c) Wipe and purge d) Texture
>
> **Q3.** True or false: milk is stretched before it is textured.

Score all six dimensions from 1 to 5: Bloom's Alignment, Objective Congruence, Item Construction Quality, Validity Evidence, Reliability Considerations, Inclusivity & Fairness. Then name any severity flags.

### Exercise 2: a slide (archetype 04, Multimedia Design)

One slide from a narrated onboarding module:

> **Slide 7, "Password policy".** The slide shows a 140-word paragraph of policy text. The narrator reads the same paragraph word for word. Beside the text is a stock photo of a padlock. Background music plays under the narration. The policy has five steps, but they are written as running prose with no numbers or bold. The narration starts when the text appears.

Score two dimensions: Multimedia Principle Compliance and Extraneous Load Reduction. For the second, name each of the five principles and say whether the slide applies it, violates it, or gives it nothing to act on.

### Exercise 3: a training request (archetype 07, Needs Analysis)

A one-paragraph needs analysis from a sales director:

> "Our close rate fell from 31% to 26% in the third quarter. The reps need better negotiation skills. I recommend a two-day negotiation workshop for all 40 reps in October."

You also happen to know that in July the company moved to a new CRM and changed the commission plan.

Score two dimensions: Cause Analysis and Intervention Appropriateness. Then say what you would ask for before anyone designs a workshop.

## Part 2: Compare your scores

A difference of one point on a dimension is normal. A difference of two or more means you and the rubric are reading the anchor differently, so re-read that dimension's anchors before moving on.

### Exercise 1: the barista quiz

| Dimension | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Bloom's Alignment | 5 | The objective asks for recall (list), and all three items are recall. The rubric scores the match, not how high the items reach. |
| Objective Congruence | 3 | Every item relates to the objective, but none asks for all four steps in order, and the items ask the learner to select where the objective says list. |
| Item Construction Quality | 2 | Q2 offers both "Purge the wand" and "Wipe and purge", so two answers are defensible. Q3 is a coin flip. |
| Validity Evidence | 2 | There is no blueprint, and the items sample only the first step, the last step, and one pair. The one objective is partly covered, which keeps this from a 1, but the full sequence it names is never tested. |
| Reliability Considerations | 1 | The quiz covers one objective, so the 10-item minimum is not the test; the test is whether the items exhaust the objective, and they do not. Add one difficulty level and no answer key (which alone holds this dimension to 2 at most). |
| Inclusivity & Fairness | 5 | Plain language; the only jargon is the job's own vocabulary. |

**Overall:** 3.0, which is "revise before wide rollout".

**Flags:** Ambiguous Correct Answers (Q2), Insufficient Item Count (the items do not exhaust the single objective), Missing or Wrong Answer Key.

**The common mistake:** scoring Bloom's Alignment low because the quiz is "only recall". It is only recall because the objective is only recall. If you think baristas should be able to steam milk, not list the steps, that is a problem with the objective, and archetype 07 or 10 is where it shows up.

**The fix:** one ordering item that asks for all four steps in sequence would test the objective directly. Then fix Q2's overlapping options and supply a key.

### Exercise 2: the password slide

| Dimension | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Multimedia Principle Compliance | 1 | The only image is a stock photo that teaches nothing. The anchor for 1 is "only decorative images; no instructional graphics". |
| Extraneous Load Reduction | 2 | Three of the five principles are violated, which is the anchor for 2. |

The five principles on this slide:

- **Coherence: violated.** The padlock photo and the music add nothing to the policy.
- **Redundancy: violated.** The narration repeats the on-screen paragraph word for word.
- **Signaling: violated.** Five steps are buried in prose with no numbers or emphasis.
- **Spatial contiguity: not in play.** There is no instructional graphic to place words beside, so there is nothing to apply or violate.
- **Temporal contiguity: applied.** The narration and the text it matches arrive together.

**The common mistake:** treating the photo as a point in the slide's favor because "it has a visual". A decorative image is a cost, not a visual.

**The fix:** replace the paragraph with the five steps as a numbered list of a few words each, let the narration carry the detail, and drop the photo and the music.

### Exercise 3: the negotiation workshop

| Dimension | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cause Analysis | 1 | There is none. The memo goes from a number to a skill gap in one sentence and does not separate environmental causes from behavior gaps. |
| Intervention Appropriateness | 1 | Training is the default, no other option is considered, and the evidence is one person's opinion. |

**Flag:** training recommended with no cause analysis, for a gap that started when two things in the environment changed.

**What to ask for first:** close rates by rep before and after July. If the reps who closed well before still do, the drop is uneven and may be a skill gap in part of the team. If everyone dropped together in July, look at the CRM and the commission plan, which training will not fix. Also ask where in the sales cycle deals are now being lost.

**The common mistake:** giving Cause Analysis a 2 or 3 because the memo names a cause. Naming a cause is not analysis. The anchor for 1 is "None; assumes training".

## Part 3: Come back to it

One reading fades within days. These short checks bring the ideas back at widening intervals. Answer from memory first, then check. It takes two or three minutes each time.

### One day later: recall

1. Name GUIDE's three quality outcomes and what each one aligns instruction with.
2. Name the four verdicts and the overall score where each one begins.
3. What two things must a scorecard include besides the scores?

### Three days later: apply

4. An objective says "analyze", and every item on the test asks for a definition. Which dimension of which archetype catches this, and what does it score?
5. Someone pastes a single quiz item and asks for a quick review. Which two dimensions have a ceiling, what is it, and which flags do not apply?
6. A narrator reads the on-screen text word for word. Which principle is violated, and which dimension scores it?

### One week later: transfer

7. A course scores 5 on needs analysis, sequencing, and assessment separately, but its objective says "apply" and its test measures recall. Which archetype catches this, and what happens to the overall?
8. Pick something of your own: a quiz, a lesson plan, a slide deck. Score it against one archetype before you ask GUIDE to. Then run `/guide-review` (or ask Claude to review it with GUIDE) and compare. Re-read the anchors for the dimension where you differed most.

## Answers to the review questions

1. **Effective:** aligned with theory, research, and documented best practice. **Efficient:** objectives, instructional strategies, and assessments aligned with each other. **Engaging:** aligned with learners' personal and professional goals.
2. **Ship** from 4.5, **light revision** from 3.5, **revise** from 2.5, **redesign** below 2.5.
3. The evidence behind each score, quoted or pointed to in the artifact, and a concrete fix for every dimension under 4. Severity flags come with it when a critical issue is present.
4. Bloom's Alignment in archetype 02, Assessment Design. It scores 1, with the flags Assessment-Objective Misalignment and Missing Bloom's Higher Order.
5. Validity Evidence and Reliability Considerations have a ceiling of 3 for a sample. The Insufficient Item Count and Coverage Gaps flags do not apply, and neither does the answer-key flag when the sample simply leaves its key out.
6. The redundancy principle, scored under Extraneous Load Reduction in archetype 04, Multimedia Design.
7. Archetype 10, Curriculum Alignment. Objective and assessment coherence scores 1 or 2, which caps the overall at 3.0 however strong the other scores are.
8. There is no single answer. The point is the comparison: the dimension where you and GUIDE differ most is the anchor to re-read.

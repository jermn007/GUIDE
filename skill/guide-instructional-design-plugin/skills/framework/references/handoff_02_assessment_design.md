# Archetype 2: Assessment Design Quality
## Handoff Documentation

**Purpose**: Design and evaluate quiz items, test blueprints, rubrics, and other assessment instruments for pedagogical soundness and measurement validity. The dimensions below work in both directions: author items against them, or score existing ones.

**When this is skipped** (an illustrative scenario): A nurse passes the medication-safety quiz with 95% and makes a dosing error the next week. The quiz asked her to define terms; the job asks her to calculate under pressure. Nobody lied, and the score was still wrong about her. If anyone will make a decision from your test results - a pass, a certification, a promotion - this archetype is what makes those results mean what you think they mean.

**Design targets** (using these dimensions forward, in design mode) - the moves that produce a 4-5:
- Write each item to its objective's Bloom level and verb - don't test recall for an "analyze" objective.
- Build a coverage blueprint (objectives x items) before writing items.
- Craft plausible, homogeneous distractors with one unambiguous key; screen every item for bias and accessibility.
- Ship the answer key (and a rubric for anything scored by judgment) with the items, and say how scores will be read: against a fixed standard (criterion-referenced) or against other test-takers (norm-referenced).

Then run an evaluate-mode self-check against the scoring anchors below.

**Professional alignment** (the professional standards this archetype *aims* to align with - an independent, aspirational mapping by the author, not reviewed or endorsed by these bodies; see [`discipline_alignment_crosswalk.md`](discipline_alignment_crosswalk.md) for the full mapping, citations, and licensing notes):
- **IBSTPI ID Competencies (2012)** - Design & Development: C16 (designing learning assessment).
- **ATD Capability Model (2020)** - Evaluating Impact; Instructional Design.
- **ISPI/HPT Ten Standards** - S10 Evaluate Results and Measure Impact (partial; assessment feeds evaluation).

**Use Cases**:
- Peer review of quiz items before deployment
- Validation of unit tests against learning objectives
- Rubric quality assurance
- Assessment blueprint gap analysis
- Item bank audits

## Evaluation Dimensions

| Dimension | Scale | Definition | Key Criteria |
|-----------|-------|------------|--------------|
| **Bloom's Alignment** | 1-5 | Does each item sit at the cognitive level its objective calls for? | Anderson et al. (2001) Revised Taxonomy: Remember → Understand → Apply → Analyze → Evaluate → Create; Knowledge types: Factual, Conceptual, Procedural, Metacognitive |
| **Objective Congruence** | 1-5 | Do items match stated learning objectives? | Mager (1997): behavior + condition + criterion; Dick/Carey (2015) alignment methodology |
| **Item Construction Quality** | 1-5 | Are stems clear, distractors plausible, items unambiguous? | Kubiszyn & Borich (2013) standards: clear stems, homogeneous distractors, single correct answer, no trick questions |
| **Validity Evidence** | 1-5 | Does the assessment show content validity through domain sampling? | Test blueprint existence and adherence; coverage of full domain or key objectives |
| **Reliability Considerations** | 1-5 | Are there enough items, appropriate difficulty, consistent scoring? | Item count (15+ norm-ref., 10-15 criterion-ref.); difficulty spread; clear rubric descriptors |
| **Inclusivity & Fairness** | 1-5 | Are items free from bias, accessible, using inclusive language? | No stereotypes, cultural bias, ableist language, gendered pronouns; accessible for diverse learners |

## Scoring Guide

### Bloom's Alignment
- **5**: Every item sits at the cognitive level its objective's verb calls for; where objectives call for higher-order thinking, items genuinely require Analyze/Evaluate/Create
- **4**: Most items match their objective's level; one or two sit a level too low or too high
- **3**: About half the items match; higher-order objectives are mostly tested at Apply or below
- **2**: Most items sit below their objective's level (recall or recognition items for Apply-and-above objectives)
- **1**: All or nearly all items are pure recall while the objectives call for more; no item reaches the level the objectives state

Score the match, not the height. A recall item for a "list" or "identify" objective is aligned and can score 5; a recall item for an "apply" objective cannot. When no objectives are stated, judge the levels against the assessment's stated purpose and say so in the rationale; the missing objectives themselves are scored under Objective Congruence.

### Objective Congruence
- **5**: Clear, measurable Mager-style objectives; every item maps to a specific objective
- **4**: Well-defined objectives; most items align; 1-2 items tangential
- **3**: Objectives present but somewhat vague; ~70% item alignment
- **2**: Vague objectives; significant alignment gaps
- **1**: Missing or unclear objectives; items don't test stated learning targets

When objective quality and item mapping point to different scores, score the mapping: well-written objectives cannot lift items that do not test them above 2. A format mismatch at the same cognitive level (the objective says list or produce, the item asks the learner to select) is scored here, not under Bloom's Alignment.

### Item Construction Quality
- **5**: Clear stems; plausible, homogeneous distractors (ideally drawn from common misconceptions); no ambiguity or trick wording
- **4**: Generally clear; minor stem revision needed; distractors adequate
- **3**: Some stems could be clearer; acceptable distractors; minor ambiguity
- **2**: Confusing stems; weak distractors; one or two trick questions
- **1**: Unclear stems; implausible distractors; pervasive ambiguity

### Validity Evidence
- **5**: Clear test blueprint; balanced coverage of domain; items represent key content
- **4**: Blueprint exists; minor coverage gaps
- **3**: Some blueprint or coverage evidence; skewed toward certain topics
- **2**: Minimal blueprint; gaps in coverage
- **1**: No blueprint; narrow coverage; misses critical content areas

### Reliability Considerations
- **5**: Sufficient items (15+ norm-ref., 10-15 criterion-ref.); varied difficulty; clear scoring rules
- **4**: Adequate items; mostly appropriate difficulty; good rubric clarity
- **3**: Minimal but acceptable item count; uneven difficulty; adequate scoring guidance
- **2**: Too few items or skewed difficulty; vague scoring
- **1**: Insufficient items; all same difficulty; no scoring consistency rules

The item counts are for an instrument that covers a module or unit. For a check on one narrow objective, judge whether the items exhaust what the objective asks (every step, every case), state that the scope is a single objective, and raise the Insufficient Item Count flag only when they do not. A complete assessment with no answer key or scoring guide scores no higher than 2 here: nothing shows that two scorers would agree. Judge item sets on split-half and internal-consistency principles, and rubrics on inter-rater reliability (clear, distinct descriptors that two scorers would apply the same way).

### Inclusivity & Fairness
- **5**: Accessible language; no bias; considers diverse learners (ELL, neurodivergent, disabilities)
- **4**: Mostly neutral; one item could be more inclusive
- **3**: Generally acceptable; 2-3 items with mild bias or accessibility concerns
- **2**: Several items with cultural bias or inaccessible language
- **1**: Pervasive bias, stereotypes, ableist language, or cultural insensitivity

### Criterion-referenced or norm-referenced?
The item-count thresholds depend on how scores will be read, so settle this before scoring Reliability Considerations:
- **Criterion-referenced**: each learner is compared to a fixed standard. Signals: a cut score or mastery criterion ("80% to pass"), items tied to stated objectives, pass/fail or certification decisions. Most quizzes, unit tests, and skill checks written for instruction are this type.
- **Norm-referenced**: learners are compared to each other. Signals: percentiles, ranks, grading on a curve, or selection and placement decisions.
- **When the artifact does not say**, treat it as criterion-referenced and state that assumption in the rationale.

### Scoring a single item or a short sample
Four dimensions can be judged from the items in hand: Bloom's Alignment, Objective Congruence, Item Construction Quality, and Inclusivity & Fairness. Validity Evidence and Reliability Considerations describe a whole instrument, so the rule depends on what the artifact claims to be:
- **A sample from a larger instrument** (one item, or an excerpt): score Validity Evidence on whether the item tests something its objective makes essential, and Reliability Considerations on whether it has one defensible key and can be scored the same way twice. 3 is the ceiling for both, not the score: an item that fails these tests scores lower on merit. Say in the rationale that blueprint coverage, item count, and difficulty spread could not be judged. Do not raise the Insufficient Item Count or Coverage Gaps flags. A pasted sample often omits its key: when one option is defensibly correct, the no-answer-key cap and the Missing or Wrong Answer Key flag do not apply, though a key that marks an indefensible option is still flagged.
- **Presented as the complete assessment**: the thresholds apply in full. One item cannot sample a domain or show a difficulty spread, so both dimensions score low and the Insufficient Item Count flag applies.
- **When you cannot tell**, ask. If you cannot ask, score it as a sample and say so.

## Severity Flags (Red Flags)

Look for and flag critical issues:

1. **Assessment-Objective Misalignment**: Items don't match learning objectives
2. **Missing Bloom's Higher Order**: All items at Remember/Understand (if objectives call for Apply+)
3. **Ambiguous Correct Answers**: More than one defensible correct answer
4. **Biased or Offensive Content**: Stereotypes, cultural insensitivity, ableist language
5. **Insufficient Item Count**: Fewer than 10 items for criterion-referenced; fewer than 15 for norm-referenced. Applies to a complete assessment covering a module or unit, not to a sample or to a single-objective check whose items exhaust the objective
6. **No Scoring Rubric**: Subjective assessment with no clear scoring criteria
7. **Coverage Gaps**: Critical content the objectives name is not assessed (judge from the blueprint, or from the objectives when there is none). Applies to a complete assessment, not to a sample
8. **Missing or Wrong Answer Key**: A complete assessment whose selected-response items have no key, or any key that marks an indefensible option, so the items cannot be scored consistently

## Improvement Suggestions

When scoring < 4 on any dimension, recommend specific next steps:

1. **Bloom's Alignment**: Rewrite the items that sit below (or above) their objective's level so each one matches its objective's verb; map every item to the cognitive taxonomy
2. **Objective Congruence**: Clarify learning objectives using Mager format (behavior, condition, criterion); remove items not aligned
3. **Item Construction**: Revise stems (eliminate double negatives, clarify wording); ensure distractors are plausible and homogeneous
4. **Validity Evidence**: Develop a test blueprint; audit item coverage against domain; rebalance if skewed
5. **Reliability**: Add items to reach minimum count; adjust difficulty for variety; supply the answer key with a rationale for each distractor; create/refine scoring rubric
6. **Inclusivity**: Review for bias (gender, culture, ability); test with diverse learner groups; use plain, accessible language

## Troubleshooting

| Issue | Resolution |
|-------|-----------|
| Scores are consistently high (4.5+) | Your items may be well-designed, or the prompt is being too lenient. Test on intentionally poor items to validate the rubric is discriminating. |
| Scores vary wildly between runs | Temperature may be too high (use 0.0). Ensure your input is formatted consistently. Consider re-running with a clearer artifact. |
| Judge flags too many "severity_flags" | Reduce sensitivity by clarifying what "severity" means in your context. A lower Bloom's level is not always severe if objectives allow it. |
| The artifact is a single item or a short excerpt | Follow "Scoring a single item or a short sample" in the scoring guide: four dimensions score normally; Validity Evidence and Reliability Considerations are capped at 3 for a sample, and the item-count and coverage flags do not apply. |
| Unclear whether the test is criterion- or norm-referenced | Look for a cut score or mastery criterion (criterion) versus percentiles, ranks, or a curve (norm). With no signal, assume criterion-referenced and state the assumption. |
| A recall quiz scores low on Bloom's Alignment although its objectives only ask for recall | It should not. Bloom's Alignment scores the match between each item and its objective, not how high the items reach. |
| Dimension scores disagree with expert judgment | Review the dimension definitions and scoring guide. Adjust prompt wording or provide reference examples to calibrate the judge. |

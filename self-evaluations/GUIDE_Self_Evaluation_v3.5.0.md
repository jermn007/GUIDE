# GUIDE Framework - Self-Evaluation v3.5.0

## The framework as shipped, scored against the rubrics as they are written today

**Date:** November 2026
**Method:** Each applicable archetype's scoring anchors, read in full from the current `archetypes/handoff_NN_*.md`, applied to the artifact. Every dimension was re-scored from evidence; no v3.4.0 score was carried forward without checking that its evidence still holds.
**Purpose:** Re-score after the v3.5.0 skill restructure (`EVALUATE.md`, `DESIGN.md`, the skill-edition rubrics, three typed commands), the Assessment Design (02) rubric edit, the regenerated printable rubric, and the new practice page and diagrams - and test whether the earlier scores survive a strict reading of the anchors.

---

## A note on the artifact being evaluated

Earlier self-evaluations scored one surface at a time: v3.1.1 through v3.3.0 scored the **living framework** (handoffs, skill, README); v3.0.0 and v3.4.0 scored the **printable `GUIDE_Rubric_Document.docx`**.

Both ship in v3.5.0, so this evaluation scores **both together** - the framework as a user receives it:

- `archetypes/handoff_NN_*.md` - the ten canonical rubrics (single source of truth)
- `skill/guide-instructional-design/` - `SKILL.md` (router), `EVALUATE.md`, `DESIGN.md`, `references/00_archetype_index.md`, and the skill-edition handoffs, plus the identical plugin mirror
- the three plugin commands: `evaluate`, `build`, `pipeline`
- `README.md`, `STATUS.md`, the plugin `README.md`, and `docs/practice.md`
- `GUIDE_Rubric_Document.docx` (stamped 3.5.0, archetype sections now generated from the handoffs)

One rule follows from scoring two surfaces: **where the living layer and the docx differ, the weaker one sets the score** for dimensions about accuracy, consistency, accessibility, and load. A reader who holds only the docx, or only the skill, gets what that surface says.

Conventions are unchanged from prior self-evaluations: six archetypes apply to GUIDE itself (01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 09); archetype 10 is scored and reported separately; 06, 07, and 08 score artifact types GUIDE is not and are N/A.

This evaluation ran seven times during the release, and each pass led to changes.

1. **First pass** scored the working tree as first prepared: 4.0. It found a dangling pointer in `EVALUATE.md`, agent lists that summed to 14, stale counts in the docx, and a wrong citation year, among others.
2. **Second pass** re-read those fixes: 4.1.
3. **Third pass** re-read a larger round of work: the docx's archetype sections regenerated from the handoffs, source names traced or removed, new edge cases and an edge-case checker, live command runs, and anchor and severity-flag edits to 04, 05, and 06. It also found that the regeneration had left hand-written front matter out of step and a print edition that repeated itself: 4.1 (4.14 and 4.12 to two decimals).
4. **Fourth pass** re-read the fixes to those: 4.2 (4.19 and 4.17).
5. **Fifth pass** scored three additions that earlier passes had listed as long-standing gaps: a practice page with score-then-compare exercises, a spaced review schedule, and three new diagrams: 4.3 (4.33 and 4.29).
6. **Sixth pass** scored the validation work: one NVDA reading of the docx, a live scoring of all 68 edge cases, and live runs of the pipeline's needs-analysis stop, fail-fast gate, and revision loop. The results file was re-computed rather than taken from its summary. Result: 4.4 on the six archetypes and 4.3 with archetype 10 (4.39 and 4.33).
7. **Seventh pass** (this document) re-read the work aimed at the sixth pass's gap list: the six drifting edge cases settled, a second model's run of all 68 cases, one full pipeline build in a single run, the calibration example's overall, five added references, a "When this is skipped" scenario in every handoff, and a CI workflow. Six others were tested against the anchor for the next score up and held, each for a reason found in a file. Result: 4.4 on the six archetypes and 4.4 with archetype 10 (4.42 and 4.36).

Every change was confirmed in the file itself before a score moved. Scores here describe the repository as it stands after the seventh round.

## What changed v3.4.0 -> v3.5.0

1. **Skill restructure.** `SKILL.md` is now a short router. It sends each run to `EVALUATE.md` or `DESIGN.md`, and each of those ends in a checkable "Done when" list. The skill's `references/handoff_*.md` are a slimmer skill edition generated by `skill/build_skill.py` (judge prompts and harness notes dropped, about a fifth smaller). The canonical handoffs stay complete.
2. **Three typed commands** in the plugin (`/evaluate`, `/build`, `/pipeline`), each run live once in Claude Code, and Commands and "It's working if" sections in the plugin README.
3. **Rubric 02 edited.** Bloom's Alignment anchors now score the match between each item and its objective, not how high the items reach. New guidance covers criterion- versus norm-referenced tests, single items and short samples, single-objective checks, and what to do when objective quality and item mapping disagree. A "Missing or Wrong Answer Key" severity flag was added. The Python judge carries the same rules.
4. **Other rubric edits.** 04's Extraneous Load and Generative Processing anchors no longer overlap. 05 and 06 gained Severity Flags lists (handoff and judge). 01, 02, 04, 05 quote the four threshold bands. Small anchor clarifications moved from judge prompts into kept sections of 01, 03, 06, 08, 09, and 10.
5. **Printable rubric regenerated.** `docs/build_rubric_docx.py` rebuilds the ten archetype sections of the docx from the handoffs, adds an archetype 10 column to the coverage matrix, marks a header row on all 58 tables, sets alt text on the figure, and has a `--check` mode (it passes). Front and back matter are still written by hand. The document grew by about four fifths in text (about 103,000 to 192,000 characters, roughly 92 pages with the practice section).
6. **Sources.** The README and index now credit each archetype only with names its handoff cites (checked name by name). Handoff 09 gained five reference entries, lost an untraceable example citation, and treats the 10-20 minute attention figure as "a design rule of thumb, not a measured limit" everywhere it appears.
7. **Tests.** `run_edge_cases.py` checks all 68 edge cases offline (it passes), and `edge_cases_02.json` has four new cases for the new 02 rules. Live scoring is described in items 10 and 11.
8. **Diagrams.** The source line in `assets/three-alignments.svg` is now #475569 (7.24:1 on its background). Three new diagrams sit where their ideas are explained, in the README and the docx, each with a full text alternative: `addie-map` (the ten archetypes on the ADDIE phases), `review-flow` (five steps of a review and the four verdict bands drawn to scale), and `alignment-cap` (archetype 10's three links and its cap rule, with an example).
9. **Practice page.** `docs/practice.md`, "Practice: Score This, Then Compare": three exercises (a quiz for 02, a slide for 04, a training request for 07) where the reader scores first and then opens GUIDE's scores, the reasoning, "the common mistake", and "the fix"; then a review schedule at one day (recall), three days (apply), and one week (transfer, including scoring the reader's own artifact), with answers. It is linked from the README and plugin README and printed as the closing section of the docx.
10. **Validation runs** (all 2026-11-12, recorded in `STATUS.md`). The author read the docx with NVDA and found no problems. `run_edge_cases.py --live` scored all 68 cases once with one model: 62 within tolerance of the expected overall, mean absolute drift 0.43, mean drift -0.30, no bad case ranked above a good one; six cases drift 0.8 to 1.8 and are listed as open. `/pipeline` ran its needs-analysis stop, its fail-fast gate (blocked at 1, redesigned, passed at 5), and one revision round (capped 3.0 to 4.89). Two defects those runs exposed were fixed and not re-run, and no complete build had finished in a single run.
11. **Follow-up to the sixth pass** (2026-11-13, recorded in `STATUS.md`). The six drifting cases were settled: two artifacts in `edge_cases_02.json` were rewritten, four expected-score sets were corrected, and two anchors were sharpened (08 Decision Points, 10 Strategy-Assessment Coherence). A second model scored all 68 cases and the first model re-scored four archetypes: 66 and 62 of 68 within tolerance, with eight cases drifting on one model each. One `/pipeline` build finished in a single headless run (20 minutes, 18 agent calls); it exposed one new defect in the revision prompts, which was fixed and not re-run. The docx calibration example now shows a weighted overall of 3.6 with a note, and its back-matter list gained five references. Every handoff opens with a "When this is skipped" scenario, carried by the skill references and the docx. `.github/workflows/checks.yml` runs the five offline checks.

## Reading this score against the history table

The composite is **4.4**, the same published figure as v3.4.0 (4.42 against 4.36 to two decimals). The match is mostly a coincidence of two movements in opposite directions, and a reader of the history table should separate three things.

**1. Corrections to earlier, more generous readings (six dimensions, -1 each).** These v3.4.0 scores had been carried forward from release to release as deltas. Read against the anchor text and a specific file, they did not hold, and v3.5.0 did not cause any of them. There was no spaced review anywhere, so Transfer & Retention could not be a 5. One figure in a long document was not "good use of visuals". No assistive-technology test had ever been recorded. A diagram is not emotional engagement. "Validated" had never been shown by a live run of the edge cases. And the docx's worked calibration example computes archetype 01's overall in a way the rubric says not to.

**2. Gains made by v3.5.0 (eight dimensions, +1 each).** The practice page, its review schedule, the three new diagrams, the screen-reader pass, the live edge-case runs, and the per-archetype scenarios are evidence the anchors ask for by name. Five of the gains bring a corrected score back to its v3.4.0 value, now earned (03 Transfer & Retention, 04 Multimedia Principle Compliance, 05 Robust, 09 Emotional Engagement, 09 Theory-Practice Grounding). Three go above v3.4.0 (03 Practice & Feedback, 09 5E Model Alignment, 09 Memory System Optimization).

**3. One loss caused by v3.5.0 (-1).** The pipeline commands shipped with more specified than has been confirmed live (10 Coverage Completeness). The seventh pass found the unconfirmed part smaller and still present. Archetype 10 is outside the six-archetype composite, so this affects only the "with archetype 10" figure.

The arithmetic, on the six applicable archetypes (36 dimension scores):

| | Points | Composite |
|---|---|---|
| v3.4.0 as published | 157 / 36 | 4.36 |
| v3.4.0 after the six corrections (-6 points) | 151 / 36 | 4.19 |
| v3.5.0: eight gains (+8 points) | 159 / 36 | **4.42** |

With archetype 10 the same steps run 182/42 = 4.33, then 176/42 = 4.19, then 183/42 = **4.36** (eight gains and the one loss). At one decimal the figure with archetype 10 moves from 4.3 to 4.4; the underlying change from v3.4.0 as published is one point in 42. Put plainly: scored this strictly, v3.4.0 would have read about 4.2, and v3.5.0 reads 4.4. The history table shows 4.4 twice; the second one was earned against the anchors and the first was not. The corrected v3.4.0 row is indicative: it applies today's corrections to the v3.4.0 scores and is not a re-score of the v3.4.0 artifact.

One correction remains in force (01 Accuracy & Grounding). Its stated cause, the calibration example, was fixed in the seventh round; the score holds at 4 for smaller imprecisions found in that pass, listed in the archetype 01 table.

## SUMMARY

v3.5.0 restructures the skill around a short router and two mode procedures with checkable endings, adds three typed commands, corrects a real flaw in the Assessment Design rubric, and makes the printable rubric a generated edition of the canonical handoffs instead of a hand-kept copy.

Late in the release it also added the three things every earlier self-evaluation had asked for - practice with feedback, spaced review, and more than one figure - and ran the validation none of them had: a screen-reader pass, a live scoring of every edge case, and live runs of the pipeline's gates.

Scored against the rubrics as they are written today, with both shipped surfaces in scope, the framework comes out at **4.4** on the six applicable archetypes and **4.4** with archetype 10 (4.42 and 4.36; 159/36 and 183/42). v3.4.0 published 4.4 and 4.3 (4.36 and 4.33). Five dimensions differ from v3.4.0. Three are up, all from the practice page and its review schedule. One is down because of this release: a small part of the pipeline is specified and not yet confirmed live. One is down as a correction (01 Accuracy & Grounding), where the cause first named is fixed and smaller imprecisions hold the score.

The matching headline number hides the real movement. Scored as strictly as this, v3.4.0 would have read about 4.2; v3.5.0 reads 4.4 because it earned eight points the stricter reading had taken away or never given. Some of that evidence is thin and is labelled so above: one screen reader, one run per edge case on each of two models, eight cases that drift on one model, scenarios no reader has been asked about. Across seven passes, the evaluation found defects, most were fixed before release, and the scores followed the evidence in both directions. The seventh pass is an example: the work it reviewed moved one score, and six others were tested against the next anchor up and held, because where a named gap closed a smaller one was found beside it. What remains is short and specific: a build that exercises the last two agents and the revision fix, repeated scoring of the edge cases, an exact statement of the tolerance, a complete reference list, a second assistive technology, and practice for more than three archetypes.
